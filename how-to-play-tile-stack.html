Beginner Guide

How to Play Tile Stack

Everything a new player needs: the rules, the slots, the tools, and a first-move routine that prevents most early losses.

By Rolin3 · September 16, 2026 · 6 min read

If you have just opened Tile Stack for the first time, this guide will walk you through everything you need to know, from your very first tap to consistently clearing full boards. I am the developer of the game, so everything below reflects exactly how the game actually works — no guessing, no second-hand tips.

The Core Idea in One Sentence

Tap open tiles to collect them, gather three identical tiles to clear them, and empty the entire board before your seven slots fill up.

That is the whole game. What makes it interesting is that the order in which you remove tiles matters more than which tiles you remove. A tile buried under two other tiles cannot be tapped at all. Every move you make either frees up future moves or quietly consumes the space you will need later.

The Board, the Slots, and the Rules

The stacked board

Tiles are arranged in overlapping layers. A tile is open only when no other tile sits on top of it. Open tiles can be tapped; covered tiles cannot. As you remove tiles from the top, the tiles underneath are gradually revealed.

Your seven slots

At the bottom of the screen you have a row of seven slots. Every tile you tap moves into a slot. When three identical tiles meet in the slots, they clear automatically and free up their space. If all seven slots fill with tiles that do not form a triple, the level ends immediately.

The single most important number in the game is seven. Every decision you make should be measured against it: how many distinct tile types are currently sitting in your slots, and how much room is left before you hit the limit?

Winning and losing

You win by clearing every tile from the board. You lose the moment the seventh slot fills without forming a match. There is no timer — the game is pure planning, not speed. You can take as long as you like between moves.

Your First Five Moves: A Recommended Routine

New players often tap the first matching pair they see. That habit wins the first few levels and then silently stops working. Instead, use this routine for the first moves of any level:

  1. Scan before you tap. Look at the whole board and identify which tiles are covering the most other tiles. Those are your priority targets.
  2. Count the visible triples. Before moving, check whether your slots already hold one or two tiles of the type you are about to collect. Collecting toward an existing partial triple is almost always better than starting a new one.
  3. Prefer central and lower tiles. Tiles near the middle and bottom of a stack tend to unlock the most material. Clearing them early opens up the board.
  4. Keep slot types low. Try to hold at most two or three different tile types in your slots at once. If you are holding five types, you are five bad draws away from a loss.
  5. Only then tap. The scan takes ten seconds and prevents most losing positions.

The Tools: Undo, Shuffle, and the BGM Toggle

Undo

Undo reverses your last tap, returning the tile from your slots to the board. Use it the moment you notice a mis-tap, before making any follow-up move. Undo is cheapest to use early; the longer you wait, the more a single bad tile costs you.

Shuffle

Shuffle rearranges the remaining board tiles. It does not touch your slots. It is your escape hatch when the visible tiles contain nothing useful. Save it for genuine dead ends rather than spending it out of impatience — a shuffled board is only helpful if you have slot space to exploit it.

Background music

The 8-bit style BGM can be toggled from the in-game settings. Your preference is saved locally in your browser, so it persists between sessions on the same device.

Progress That Persists

Tile Stack saves your level progress, score, and settings in your browser's local storage. Close the tab, come back tomorrow, and the game resumes where you left off — as long as you return on the same browser and device. Clearing your browser data resets your progress, so keep that in mind before running any cleanup tools.
