Tile Stack vs. Mahjong Solitaire

Layered tiles, matching, board clearing — cousins indeed. But the seven-slot tray changes everything.

By Rolin3 · September 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Players who remember the classic computer era often ask whether Tile Stack is "a kind of Mahjong Solitaire." The two games look like cousins — layered tiles, matching, board clearing — and they genuinely are related. But the differences change how each game feels and how you win. Here is a precise comparison.

The Shared DNA

Both games descend from the same idea formalized in 1980s computer Mahjong Solitaire:

  • Tiles are arranged in layered layouts that gate access by position.
  • Only free tiles can be taken; covered tiles wait.
  • The win condition is clearing the entire board, not scoring.
  • There is no timer in either game's core mode. Planning beats speed.

If you were good at Mahjong Solitaire twenty years ago, the "read what a tile covers before you take it" instinct will transfer directly — it is the same skill the genre has always tested.

The Mechanical Differences

Where matches happen

Mahjong Solitaire: matches resolve on the board itself. You remove two free matching tiles and they are gone instantly. There is no holding area. Tile Stack: matches resolve in the seven-slot tray. Taken tiles wait in the tray until three gather. This single difference creates everything else below.

The loss condition

Mahjong Solitaire: you lose access, not the game — if no free match remains, you reshuffle or restart. There is no accumulating pressure. Tile Stack: the tray is a countdown. Seven non-matching tiles ends the level. The game has a real, visible failure state that your earlier decisions built.

Matching constraint

Mahjong Solitaire: pairs, with additional "free on at least one side" rules. Tile Stack: triples, any position, no side rules — but the tray tax means you can never take a tile for free; every take occupies space until its partners arrive.

Recovery tools

Mahjong Solitaire: typically shuffle-when-stuck. Tile Stack: two distinct tools with different jobs — Undo for personal mistakes, Shuffle for genuinely dried-up boards — both free and unlimited, as covered in the tools guide.

The Feel: Two Different Tensions

Mahjong Solitaire is a survey game: the tension is finding the next legal move in a slowly diminishing landscape. It is contemplative, almost meditative, and a mistake costs options rather than existence.

Tile Stack is a budget game: the tension is that every take spends space you cannot get back until a triple clears. It is tighter, more sequential, and each level is a compressed story about a budget spent well or badly. The seven-slot tray — the number the whole game hangs on, as explained in the design notes — makes that budget visible at all times.

Which One Teaches Which Skill

Skill Mahjong Solitaire Tile Stack
Visual search Heavy — scan for any free pair Medium — scan for chosen types
Sequencing / planning Medium Heavy — the core skill
Resource discipline Light Heavy — slots are a budget
Consequence memory Low stakes High stakes — losses are specific

Playing Both Makes You Better at Both

The transfer is real: Mahjong Solitaire sharpens the coverage-reading eye that Tile Stack's strategy guide is built on, and Tile Stack's slot discipline makes Mahjong's occasional pressure moments feel easy. If you have history with the older game, Tile Stack will feel familiar within one level and newly demanding within three — which is, frankly, the intended experience.
